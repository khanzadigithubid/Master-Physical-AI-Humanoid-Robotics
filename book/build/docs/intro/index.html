Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics

An AI-Native Textbook for Embodied Intelligence​

Welcome to the comprehensive university-level textbook on Physical AI and Humanoid Robotics. This textbook bridges the gap between artificial intelligence and robotics, focusing on embodied agents that perceive, reason, and act in the physical world.

What is Physical AI?​

Physical AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that exist and operate in the physical world through robotic embodiments. Unlike pure software AI (like ChatGPT or image generators), Physical AI must:

  • Perceive the real world through sensors (cameras, LiDAR, tactile sensors)
  • Reason about 3D space, physics, and object interactions
  • Act through actuators to manipulate objects and navigate environments
  • Learn from real-world experience, not just data

Physical AI is the convergence of:

  • Robotics: Mechanics, control systems, sensors, actuators
  • Computer Vision: Visual perception, 3D reconstruction, object recognition
  • Machine Learning: Reinforcement learning, imitation learning, foundation models
  • Control Theory: Feedback control, optimization, trajectory planning

Why Humanoid Robotics?​

Humanoid robots—robots with human-like body structure—are the ultimate test of Physical AI because:

  1. Human Environments: Our world is designed for human morphology (stairs, doorknobs, chairs)
  2. Rich Manipulation: Two arms with dexterous hands enable complex tasks
  3. Bipedal Locomotion: Walking on two legs is dynamically unstable but highly versatile
  4. Human-Robot Interaction: Human-like form enables natural communication and collaboration

Companies like Tesla (Optimus), Figure AI, 1X Technologies, and Agility Robotics are racing to deploy humanoid robots for warehouses, factories, and homes.

Course Structure​

This textbook is organized into 6 modules with 24 chapters:

Module 1: Introduction to Physical AI​

  • Overview of the field
  • Physical AI vs. traditional AI
  • Curriculum guide and prerequisites

Module 2: Robotics Fundamentals​

  • Kinematics (forward and inverse)
  • Dynamics and equations of motion
  • Control theory (PID, LQR, MPC)
  • Actuators and sensors

Module 3: Perception Systems​

  • Computer vision for robotics
  • LiDAR and depth sensors
  • Sensor fusion (Kalman filters, EKF)
  • SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)

Module 4: AI for Robotics​

  • Reinforcement learning for control
  • Imitation learning and behavior cloning
  • World models and predictive learning
  • Foundation models for robotics (RT-X, VIMA)

Module 5: Humanoid Robotics​

  • Bipedal locomotion and gait planning
  • Manipulation and grasping
  • Human-robot interaction
  • Whole-body control

Module 6: Deployment & Ethics​

  • Sim-to-real transfer
  • Safety systems and fail-safes
  • Ethical considerations
  • Future directions

Learning Objectives​

By the end of this textbook, you will be able to:

✅ Model robot kinematics and dynamics mathematically ✅ Design control systems for robotic manipulators ✅ Implement computer vision pipelines for perception ✅ Train reinforcement learning agents in simulation ✅ Build complete Physical AI systems from scratch ✅ Evaluate safety and ethics of deployed robots

Prerequisites​

This is a university-level textbook (junior/senior undergraduate or graduate level). We assume:

Required Background​

  • Linear Algebra: Matrices, vectors, eigenvalues, SVD
  • Calculus: Derivatives, gradients, optimization
  • Probability: Bayesian inference, Gaussian distributions
  • Programming: Python (NumPy, PyTorch/TensorFlow)

Recommended Background​

  • Physics: Newtonian mechanics, dynamics
  • Control Theory: Feedback systems, PID controllers
  • Machine Learning: Neural networks, gradient descent

If you need to brush up on these topics, see our Prerequisites Chapter.
